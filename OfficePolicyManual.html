Advisor Assistant Policy

A Growth Partner Advisor may engage an assistant — licensed or unlicensed — to support their practice. The type of assistant determines what activities they may perform, what firm systems they may access, and what compliance obligations apply. In both cases, broker approval is required before the assistant may begin work or access any DBRE® systems, and the sponsoring Advisor assumes full responsibility for the assistant's conduct.

Licensed Assistants

A licensed assistant holds an active NC real estate license and may perform a broader scope of support activity under the Advisor's supervision.

Permitted activities — Licensed Assistants
· Upload and organize transaction documents in the sponsoring Advisor's Boardroom
· Coordinate scheduling, communications, and administrative follow-up
· Accompany the Advisor at showings or open houses in a support role, with disclosure
· Prepare and distribute Advisor-approved marketing materials
· Perform transaction coordination support under the Advisor's direct supervision

Prohibited activities — Licensed Assistants
· Independently hold listings or enter agency agreements
· Represent clients in negotiations without the Advisor's direct supervision
· Host open houses alone or conduct showings independently
· Receive direct commission payments from DBRE®

Any client-facing activity by the assistant must be disclosed as such. A licensed assistant may not conduct any activity that would constitute independent practice.
Digital Workspace Access — Licensed Assistants
· General — firm-wide announcements and updates
· The Café — team community channel
· Knowledge Vault — read access to SOPs, policies, and forms
· Sponsoring Advisor's Private Boardroom — full access for file uploads and transaction documents
· The Closing Table — transaction workflow support
· The Content Studio — marketing content and creative assets

No access to: The Network, The Message Center, The Creative Suite, The Conference Room, The Achievement Circle, or any other Advisor's Boardroom.
Unlicensed Assistants

An unlicensed assistant performs administrative and clerical support only. NCREC rules strictly prohibit unlicensed individuals from performing any activity that requires a real estate license. Violation of this boundary is a license violation for the sponsoring Advisor and may expose the firm to disciplinary action.

Permitted activities — Unlicensed Assistants
· Administrative and clerical work: scheduling, data entry, filing, phone coordination
· Managing the Advisor's calendar and coordinating with vendors, inspectors, or contractors
· Delivering or picking up documents (not explaining or negotiating their contents)
· Attending open houses only when a licensed Advisor is present — may greet visitors but may not answer substantive questions about the property, price, or terms
· Social media management: posting pre-approved content only, not creating property representations
· Document uploading and deadline tracking support — not advising any party

Prohibited activities — Unlicensed Assistants
· Showing property independently under any circumstances
· Discussing or negotiating price, terms, or contract language with any party
· Hosting an open house alone
· Soliciting listings or buyer clients
· Answering substantive questions about a property's value, condition, or market
· Signing any real estate document on behalf of the Advisor or a client
· Receiving any compensation tied to a specific transaction — may receive a salary or hourly wage only, never a commission split or referral fee
Digital Workspace Access — Unlicensed Assistants
· General — firm-wide announcements and updates
· The Café — team community channel
· The Content Studio — marketing content and creative assets

No access to: Knowledge Vault, Sponsoring Advisor's Boardroom, The Closing Table, The Network, The Message Center, The Creative Suite, The Conference Room, The Achievement Circle, or any other Advisor's Boardroom. Unlicensed assistants are not permitted access to transaction files or compliance documents.
Additional Workspace Seat Fee — Both Types
The sponsoring Advisor pays an additional $50/month per assistant seat (licensed or unlicensed), added to the standard $150/month platform fee. The seat fee applies for each month the assistant seat is active, regardless of transaction activity.

To add an assistant seat, notify the Operations Specialist in writing with the assistant's name, license number (if applicable), assistant type (licensed or unlicensed), and intended start date. The Operations Specialist will configure the appropriate access level and the CFO will confirm the fee adjustment.
Compliance — Both Types: The sponsoring Advisor assumes full responsibility for the assistant's conduct. Any violation of NCREC rules or DBRE® policy by the assistant is treated as a violation by the Advisor and may result in corrective action up to and including termination of the ICA. Notify the Principal Broker and Operations Specialist within 48 hours of any change in the assistant's engagement, including departure, license lapse (licensed assistants), or any circumstance requiring removal of system access.

Referral Fee Policy

Dana Blanks Real Estate® operates two referral channels. Each has its own rules.

Channel 1 — Internal: Track B Referral Partners referring to Growth Partner Advisors
Advisors affiliated with DBRE Referral Partners, LLC (Track B) may refer prospective buyers or sellers to active Growth Partner Advisors (Track A) under Dana Blanks Real Estate, Inc. The referral fee is a fixed 25% of the gross commission received by Dana Blanks Real Estate, Inc. on the closed transaction. This rate is set in the Referral Partner Agreement and is not subject to Advisor discretion. The referral fee is paid to Dana Blanks Real Estate, Inc., which then disburses the Track B Advisor's portion per their Referral Partner Agreement.
Channel 2 — External: Growth Partner Advisors referring to or receiving from outside firms
When a Growth Partner Advisor refers a client to a broker at an outside firm — or receives a referral from an outside firm — the standard outbound referral fee is 25% of the gross commission received by the receiving brokerage. Advisors have discretion to negotiate up to 30% based on referral quality or market conditions. Rates above 30% require broker approval. All external referral fees are paid to Dana Blanks Real Estate, Inc. — not directly to the Advisor — and are governed by a written referral agreement executed by both brokers of record before the referral is made.

Incoming external referral fees: When DBRE receives a referral fee from an outside firm on a transaction, the agreed referral fee is deducted from the gross commission before the Growth Partner Advisor's split is calculated. The Advisor must upload the incoming referral agreement to their Advisor Boardroom so the firm can track the obligation and process the deduction accurately at closing.
Key rules for all referral fee activity:
· Written referral agreement required — executed by both brokers of record before the referral is made. Individual Advisor signatures alone are not sufficient.
· All referral agreements must be reviewed and approved by the Principal Broker before execution.
· Referral fees may only be paid to or received from licensed real estate brokerages. Fees may never be paid to unlicensed individuals.
· RESPA compliance required on all referral fee transactions.

Section 5

MLS, Listing & Cooperative Compensation Standards

All listings must comply with the rules of the Advisor's Governing Association MLS (Cape Fear REALTORS® for Wilmington/Cape Fear market Advisors; Raleigh Regional Association of REALTORS® for Wake County/Triangle market Advisors) and DBRE's internal listing standards. MLS entry is handled exclusively by the BIC or Operations Specialist — Advisors submit the completed MLS Information Sheet and do not enter listings directly.

MLS Entry Requirements
!
Entry deadline: 1 business day from marketing commencement
All listings must be entered into the MLS within 1 business day of marketing commencement per Clear Cooperation (DBRE-POL-002). Any prior reference to a 3-business-day deadline is superseded. Advisors must submit the MLS Information Sheet in time to meet this deadline.
2
MLS entry by BIC or Operations Specialist only
Advisors do not enter listings into the MLS directly. All entries, status changes, and price adjustments are made by the BIC or Operations Specialist. Unauthorized MLS entries are a policy violation subject to disciplinary action.
3
Coming Soon: 30-day maximum, no showings or offers
  • No showings or offers may be accepted during Coming Soon status
  • Coming Soon may not exceed 30 days before transitioning to Active
  • Pre-approval or proof of funds required before any showing after Active status is set
SOP Note: DBRE-OPS-004 and DBRE-OPS-005 currently reference a 3-business-day MLS entry deadline. This is an error pending correction. The operative deadline is 1 business day per DBRE-POL-002 in all cases.
Listing Agreement Expiration & Renewal

A listing agreement that expires without renewal or amendment terminates the firm's authority to represent the seller immediately. Advisors are solely responsible for monitoring their own listing expiration dates.

1
Renewal or Amendment (Before Expiration)
If a listing is active and approaching expiration and the seller wishes to continue, the Advisor must submit an Agency Agreement Renewal or Amendment to the seller for execution a minimum of 10 days prior to the listing expiration date. The executed renewal or amendment must be uploaded to the Advisor Boardroom and submitted to the BIC immediately upon execution.
2
If the Listing Expires Without Renewal
The Advisor may not continue to represent the seller, market the property, or hold open houses under the DBRE® name. Marketing materials must be taken down promptly, and the Broker-in-Charge must be notified without delay. Representation may resume only once a new listing agreement is fully executed — a renewal of the expired agreement's terms is not sufficient.
3
MLS Compliance
Expired listings are updated in the MLS by the BIC or Operations Specialist immediately upon expiration. Advisors must notify the Operations Specialist of any upcoming expiration as part of the 10-day advance renewal process.

Section 8

Professional Standards & Fair Housing

DBRE Advisors are expected to conduct themselves at the highest standard of professional practice at all times — in client interactions, in the MLS, in communications with cooperating brokers, and in the communities where they work.

Code of Conduct
1
NAR Code of Ethics compliance required
All DBRE Advisors are required to know and comply with the NAR Code of Ethics. Ethics violations are treated as policy violations and may result in termination of the ICA.
2
Fair housing — zero tolerance
DBRE has a zero-tolerance policy for any conduct that violates the Fair Housing Act or its NC equivalents. This includes steering, discriminatory advertising language, selective disclosure, or any differential treatment based on race, color, national origin, religion, sex, familial status, disability, or any other protected class.
3
Professional communication standards
  • Negative commentary about other brokerages, Advisors, or firms is strictly prohibited in any written or verbal form
  • Disputes with cooperating parties are handled through proper channels — not social media or group chats
  • Social media posts involving DBRE, clients, or transactions must comply with DBRE-POL-007
  • Tagging conventions, hashtag standards, and content formatting are governed by the DBRE Brand Standards Guide — see current version for specifics
4
Agency disclosure — required at first substantial contact
Advisors must provide the Working with Real Estate Agents brochure and make proper agency disclosure at first substantial contact with all buyers and sellers. Agency disclosure must be documented in the transaction file. North Carolina is an agency disclosure state — this requirement is non-negotiable.
Agency Relationships

Dana Blanks Real Estate® operates under the following defined agency relationships. Advisors must understand and be able to explain each relationship to clients and customers clearly and accurately.

1
Buyer Agency
DBRE represents buyers as clients. All buyer representation requires a signed Exclusive Buyer Representation Agreement before any property is shown (see Section 7). As a buyer's agent, the Advisor owes full fiduciary duties to the buyer — loyalty, confidentiality, disclosure, obedience, reasonable care, and accounting. The firm does not represent unrepresented buyers as clients; customers receive honest, fair dealing but not fiduciary representation.
2
Seller Agency
DBRE represents sellers as clients under a signed Exclusive Right to Sell Listing Agreement. As a seller's agent, the Advisor owes full fiduciary duties to the seller. All material facts known about the buyer must be disclosed to the seller, and the Advisor must work to achieve the best possible outcome for the seller throughout the transaction.
3
Dual Agency
Dual agency arises when a DBRE Advisor represents both the buyer and seller in the same transaction. Dual agency is permitted at DBRE only with informed written consent from both parties before proceeding. Upon identification of a potential dual agency situation, the Advisor must notify the Principal Broker immediately and obtain written consent from both parties before continuing. Dual agency limits the Advisor's ability to advocate fully for either party — this must be explained clearly to both clients. Dual agency transactions qualify for the enhanced split (see Section 3).
4
Designated Agency
Designated agency occurs when the Principal Broker designates one Advisor to represent the seller and a different Advisor to represent the buyer in the same transaction, avoiding the limitations of dual agency. DBRE practices designated agency where possible as the preferred alternative to dual agency when two DBRE Advisors are involved in the same transaction. Each designated Advisor may provide full fiduciary representation to their respective client. The Principal Broker, as the appointing broker, remains a dual agent but does not represent either party to the disadvantage of the other. Designated agency requires written disclosure to and consent from both parties.
All four agency relationships — buyer agency, seller agency, dual agency, and designated agency — must be disclosed in writing. The Working with Real Estate Agents brochure satisfies initial disclosure. Agency agreements and consent forms satisfy ongoing documentation. All executed agency documents must be retained in the transaction file per Section 10.

Crisis & Reputation Management
The First Rule: Do not respond publicly. Escalate to the Broker-in-Charge first. Always.

This policy applies whenever a negative review is posted, a client dispute becomes visible in writing, a transaction falls apart publicly, a media inquiry is received, an Advisor is mentioned negatively on social media, or any NCREC complaint or legal notice is received.

1
Do not respond publicly
Do not reply to any review, comment, post, or message in any public forum until you have spoken with the Broker-in-Charge. This includes replying to defend yourself, thank someone, or clarify facts. Even a polite response can escalate a situation or create a legal record that limits your options.
2
Notify the Broker-in-Charge within 24 hours
Contact the Broker-in-Charge as soon as you become aware of the incident. Provide the full context: what happened, when, what was said, who is involved, and where it is visible. Text or call first; follow up with an email summary. Do not wait until you have all the answers — notify first, gather details second.
3
Document everything immediately
Screenshot the review, post, or communication before taking any other action. Public posts can be edited or deleted. Save the screenshot to your private Boardroom. If the incident involves a transaction, compile the relevant correspondence and file notes.
4
Work with the Broker-in-Charge on the response
The Broker-in-Charge will determine the appropriate response — whether that is a crafted public reply, direct private outreach to the client, no response at all, or escalation to legal counsel. No Advisor may respond publicly without the Broker-in-Charge's approval of the language.

Section 10

AI Use Policy

Artificial intelligence tools are permitted and encouraged at DBRE as production aids — the same way Canva is a tool for design, AI is a tool for drafting. What AI cannot do is replace an Advisor's judgment, voice, or professional responsibility for any content or communication published under the DBRE® name. This policy governs all AI use by DBRE Advisors across content creation, client communications, transaction documents, and operational tasks.

The Core Standard
The Core Standard: If it has your name on it, it is yours — regardless of how it was drafted. AI assistance does not reduce your responsibility for accuracy, tone, compliance, or brand alignment. It transfers that responsibility entirely to you.
Permitted Uses
✓
Content drafting
Generating a first draft of captions, emails, market updates, blog posts, or newsletters — provided the Advisor reviews, edits, and takes full ownership before publishing. The final output must reflect the Advisor's voice and the DBRE brand standard.
✓
Brainstorming & research assistance
Using AI to generate content ideas, post angles, hashtag options, or to summarize market data the Advisor has already independently verified. AI may assist with research framing — it may not be the source of facts presented as the Advisor's own analysis.
✓
Client communication drafts
Drafting inquiry responses, follow-up emails, or buyer/seller update messages — with mandatory human review before sending. Advisors must ensure all AI-drafted client communications are accurate, appropriate, and consistent with the DBRE voice before they leave the Advisor's outbox.
✓
Operational productivity
Using AI for internal tasks — organizing notes, drafting checklists, summarizing documents, preparing internal communications. These uses carry lower brand risk but the same accuracy responsibility.
Prohibited Uses
The following are prohibited under this policy. Violations are treated as professional standards violations under Section 8.

· Auto-publishing without review. No AI-generated content may be published, sent, or submitted without the Advisor reading, editing, and approving it first. Scheduled posts queued directly from AI output without human review are a violation.

· Unverified listing descriptions. AI may not be used to write or generate any part of a property listing description unless every factual claim — square footage, features, HOA status, school assignments, lot size, year built — is verified line by line against MLS data before publication. AI tools hallucinate property details. This is a compliance violation, not just a brand issue.

· Market predictions and pricing opinions. AI may not be used to generate CMA data, pricing recommendations, or market forecast statements presented as the Advisor's professional opinion. These require the Advisor's own analysis of current, verified market data.

· Legal or regulatory statements. AI may not be used to draft agency disclosure language, contract terms, addenda language, or any content that carries legal or regulatory weight. All such content must come from approved NCREC forms or be reviewed by a licensed attorney.

· Client data input into public AI tools. Client names, contact information, transaction details, property addresses, or any personally identifiable information may not be entered into public AI tools (including free-tier versions of any AI platform). Use only firm-approved tools that maintain appropriate data privacy protections.
Listing Descriptions — Compliance Requirement
Compliance Requirement — Listing Descriptions: Any listing description that involved AI assistance — even as a starting point — must be verified against MLS data before it is published anywhere. This is not a brand preference. It is a compliance requirement. AI tools routinely generate plausible-sounding but inaccurate property details. Publishing an inaccurate listing description — regardless of how it was drafted — creates NCREC disclosure and misrepresentation exposure for the Advisor and the firm. Verify every claim. Every time.
Pre-Publish Checklist — AI-Assisted Content

Before publishing or sending any AI-assisted content, Advisors must be able to answer yes to all of the following:

1
Does it sound like me?
If it reads like a generic real estate caption — "dream home," "don't miss out," "nestled in a charming neighborhood" — it is not ready. Rewrite it in your voice.
2
Is every factual claim verified?
For listing descriptions specifically: have you checked every detail against MLS data? For market content: is the data from a source you verified, not from the AI's general knowledge?
3
Is it on-brand?
Would Dana recognize this as DBRE content if she saw it without attribution? Does it reflect the brand voice standard in the DBRE Brand Standards Guide?
4
Am I comfortable owning it fully?
Your name on the content is your endorsement of it — the accuracy, the tone, and every claim. If you would not be comfortable defending it to a client, a cooperating agent, or the broker, it is not ready.
5
Does it contain any client data?
If the content originated from a prompt that included client names, contact details, or transaction-specific information, confirm that information was entered only into firm-approved, privacy-compliant tools — not public AI platforms.
Disclosure

DBRE does not require Advisors to publicly disclose AI use in published content, provided the Advisor has reviewed, edited, and taken full ownership of the final output. The Advisor's name on the content is their endorsement of its accuracy, voice, and compliance. No disclosure language is required in social media posts, emails, or marketing materials as a result of AI-assisted drafting alone.

This disclosure standard reflects current NAR guidance and industry practice as of September 2026. As regulatory standards evolve, DBRE will update this policy accordingly. Advisors are responsible for monitoring any state-specific requirements that may arise regarding AI disclosure in real estate advertising.

Policy Violations & Disciplinary Procedures

DBRE takes policy compliance seriously. The firm's policies exist to protect clients, Advisors, and the firm's license and reputation. Violations are addressed promptly, consistently, and in proportion to their severity.

1
Category 1 — Administrative violations
Failure to upload documents on time, missed CE documentation deadlines, late form submission. Addressed with a written notice and corrective action deadline. Repeated Category 1 violations escalate to Category 2.
2
Category 2 — Policy violations
Showing property without a BRA, unauthorized MLS entries, missing the MLS entry deadline, failure to obtain required written consents, or violations of the cooperative compensation policy. Results in a formal written warning and may include a remediation plan.
3
Category 3 — Serious violations
Fair housing violations, wire fraud facilitation or negligence, misrepresentation, disclosure failures, conduct that exposes the firm to license jeopardy, or repeated Category 2 violations. May result in immediate suspension and ICA termination.

1
Written notification from the BIC

2
5 business days to respond in writing
The Advisor has 5 business days to respond. The response is considered by the BIC before a final determination is made.
3
Written resolution — retained in Advisor's file
The BIC issues a written resolution. All materials related to the violation, response, and resolution are retained in the Advisor's file in the Boardroom.
